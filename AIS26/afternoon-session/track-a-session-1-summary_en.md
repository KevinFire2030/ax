## One-Sentence Summary

LG Electronics is working to connect fragmented customer behavior data across home appliances, mobility, content, and commerce through an LLM-based Behavior Intelligence Model, enabling AI to understand context and intent rather than merely automating isolated actions.

## Core Message

AI is rapidly moving beyond question answering toward agentic systems that can judge, plan, and act. However, understanding real human life and lifestyle context requires more than text-based LLM capabilities. Most enterprise data exists as fragmented events: opening a refrigerator door, turning on a TV, moving in a car, watching content, or using a service.

The speaker emphasized that a single event rarely reveals user intent by itself. Opening a refrigerator door may mean drinking water before work, preparing dinner after coming home, or casually looking for a snack before bed. Turning on a TV at 8 p.m. may also reflect very different situations depending on the household and life stage. The right experience depends on the surrounding context, not the isolated action.

LG Electronics defines `Behavior Intelligence` as the ability to infer the intent hidden behind event data by combining current situation, behavioral sequence over time, repeated patterns, habits, and memory. In this view, an action becomes behavior when seen over time, becomes context when connected to surrounding circumstances, and approaches intent when those signals are interpreted together.

## Technical Approach

LG's Behavior Intelligence Model is not positioned as a replacement for LLMs. Instead, it uses LLM architecture and reasoning capabilities to understand a different kind of data: behavior. While LLMs learn language tokens and sentence sequences, the Behavior Intelligence Model learns `Behavior Tokens` and behavior sequences.

A Behavior Token represents actions, events, product states, or service states. LG structures cross-domain data by asking who acted, with whom, what happened, when it happened, how long or how often it continued, where it happened, and why it may have happened. This gives different data domains, such as home appliances, mobility, commerce, content, IoT, and sensor data, a common language that AI can process.

The model aims not only to predict the next token, but to predict the next action, the next need, the reason behind behavior, and the proactive service or experience that should follow.

## Business Implications

The speaker stressed that Behavior Intelligence must become a platform and business layer, not just a technical concept. One example is connecting experiences across home and mobility: a person may leave home, consume content in a car, make a purchase based on that content, and return home to a connected environment.

Home-to-car and car-to-home services should evolve beyond simple device control into connected experiences. The same person needs different recommendations when commuting alone on a weekday and when traveling with a child on a weekend. LG is exploring how contextual understanding can support mobility solutions, content experiences, commerce, and advertising.

In content and advertising, the old approach of maximizing inventory exposure is no longer sufficient. LG's direction is to provide content experiences that match the household's situation and intent. The speaker also noted that instead of relying on direct identification of "who" is watching, the company is shifting toward interpreting context and preference as intent.

## Physical AI and Robotics

The session also connected Behavior Intelligence to physical AI and robotics. A robot entering the home must do more than perceive objects or perform pick-and-place tasks. It must understand what it should do, what it should not do, when to communicate with or intervene for the customer, and what must be protected from safety and security perspectives.

For that, a robot needs an understanding of life context. The speaker framed Behavior Intelligence as a key foundation for physical AI: not as intrusive hyper-personalization, but as the ability to deliver what a customer needs at the right moment.

## Key Takeaways

- Agentic AI requires an understanding of behavioral context and user intent before it can act usefully.
- Enterprise data is often fragmented into isolated events, so it must be transformed into a common behavior language that AI can understand.
- Behavior Intelligence uses LLM-like architecture while focusing on behavior tokens, temporal sequences, patterns, habits, and memory.
- The intelligence layer that connects home, mobility, content, and commerce can become a new business platform.
- Physical AI and home robots need lifestyle context and intervention criteria, not just perception and manipulation skills.
